How This Was Calculated

Inches are converted to a fraction of a foot (÷12) and added to the feet value, for each of the three dimensions. Multiplying the three converted dimensions gives the volume in cubic feet, and dividing that by 100 gives the number of units — the standard measure lorries use for sand, M-sand, and crushed stone.

What Is a "Unit" of Sand or Aggregate?

In construction, sand, M-sand, and crushed stone (jelly) are commonly measured and sold in units, where 1 unit equals 100 cubic feet. This convention is widely used across South India to price lorry loads of loose material, since suppliers deliver material heaped in a lorry rather than in bags or by weight.

Because material is often measured against an excavated pit, a stockpile, or a lorry body, dimensions are usually taken in feet and inches rather than decimal feet — which is why this calculator lets you enter each dimension as a whole number of feet plus a remainder in inches.

Step-by-Step Example

  • Length: 10 ft 6 in → 10 + (6 ÷ 12) = 10.5 ft
  • Breadth: 8 ft 0 in → 8 + (0 ÷ 12) = 8.0 ft
  • Depth: 1 ft 3 in → 1 + (3 ÷ 12) = 1.25 ft
  • Volume: 10.5 × 8.0 × 1.25 = 105 cu.ft
  • Units: 105 ÷ 100 = 1.05 units

Quick Reference

  • 1 unit = 100 cu.ft ≈ 2.83 cu.m
  • 1 foot = 12 inches
  • A standard lorry load is often quoted as a fixed number of units (e.g. "1 unit lorry", "1.5 unit lorry").
  • Always confirm depth/height carefully — it's the dimension most often measured incorrectly and has the biggest effect on the final volume.
